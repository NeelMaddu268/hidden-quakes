# Pitch, judges, Devpost, README

Every `{value}` is read off the screen at demo time, never memorized and never typed into copy. The reveal lands before any explanation of how.

## 10-second pitch

"The public regional catalog lists {publicCatalogCount} events under Utah's geothermal frontier on {windowLabel}. We rebuilt the catalog from raw public seismometers with neural phase picking, multi-station association and relocation: {candidateCount} candidate events, {strictQualityCount} at strict quality, underground where the public view is nearly empty."

## 30-second pitch

"Enhanced geothermal is expanding around Milford, Utah. Operators have dense downhole monitoring. The public gets the public regional catalog: {publicCatalogCount} events here on {windowLabel}. *(REVEAL)* Hidden Quakes pulls raw waveforms from the public seismic network, picks P and S arrivals with a neural network, associates them across stations, relocates them underground and scores every one. We recover {recoveredCatalogCount} of the {publicCatalogCount} public events, plus {additionalCount} more candidates, {strictAdditionalCount} of them strict. Click any dot and you see the waveforms that put it there."

## Hostile judge questions

Answer in one or two sentences, then show something on screen. If the honest answer is "we don't know," say it first.

| # | Question | Answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Isn't PhaseNet doing everything? | PhaseNet picks arrivals one station at a time. An event exists only when picks agree across stations through a velocity model, so association, relocation, statics, uncertainty, tiers and matching are ours, and they're most of the work. |
| 2 | Isn't this QuakeFlow? | Same family of methods, and we say so. QuakeFlow is a research workflow; we built a public-data product with reproducible runs, tiers anchored to the public catalog, evidence behind every dot, and a UI a non-seismologist can read. |
| 3 | Doesn't FORGE already have better sensors? | Yes. Downhole geophones and fiber give far denser catalogs than ours. Those belong to operators and research teams and usually arrive later. We show what the public stream supports, today. |
| 4 | Why compare to a public catalog? | It's the reference the public actually has, and our only label set. We lean on it throughout: recovering its events is our recall check, those events set our tier bars, their catalog hypocentres anchor our station timing corrections, and its magnitudes calibrate ours. So agreement with it is a consistency check, not independent proof. |
| 5 | Are the additional events real? | We call them candidate events: each needs consistent picks across multiple stations, and we don't claim all {candidateCount} are real. Strict means every quality metric is within the range reached by three-quarters of the public events we recovered, one metric at a time; only {strictJointShare} recovered public events clear every bar at once (never say three-quarters for that). {strictAdditionalCount} of the {strictQualityCount} strict events are not in the public catalog. |
| 6 | How many do you actually trust? | *(Press S.)* {strictQualityCount} at strict quality: {strictAdditionalCount} not in the public catalog, plus {strictMatchedCount} public events we recovered. |
| 7 | Why are your depths broader than published FORGE clouds? | Those use sensors much closer to the source, plus relative relocation. We use sparser public stations and absolute locations. Every halo is that event's own error. We show depths as located and read no geometry into the cloud; that would need relative relocation, which we didn't run. |
| 8 | Why care about tiny events? | Microseismicity is the public's only signal at this scale, and the oversight of geothermal operations (traffic-light protocols) is built on it. We show where and when candidate events occur; we don't say why. |
| 9 | What did you build? | Ingestion, preprocessing per sensor type, association config, our own locator with statics and uncertainty, tiering, catalog matching, the exporter, and the 3D product. |
| 10 | Did you train a neural network? | Not the picker: we used pretrained PhaseNet weights and chose between them by A/B on public-catalog events, because site labels for picking don't exist. We did train one small model of our own on this run: the {confidenceLabel}, trained on decoy events made by scrambling station clocks. Each candidate's score says how much its timing looks like a real association rather than a scrambled-clock decoy, and on held-out events the model's ROC AUC is {heldOutRocAuc}. It is not a probability that an event is an earthquake; the tiers stay the quality call, and the score is one more thing to inspect in the drawer. *(Say the decoy-test sentences only if the card shows the "(held-out ROC AUC)" row.)* |
| 11 | How is this different from STA/LTA? | STA/LTA triggers on energy ratios; PhaseNet separates P from S and works at lower signal-to-noise. On the same day, with the same association, locator and tier bars, STA/LTA gives {staltaCandidates} candidates and {strictStalta} strict, PhaseNet {strictPhasenet} strict; STA/LTA's median timing misfit is {staltaMedianRmsS} s against PhaseNet's {phasenetMedianRmsS} s, on a median of {staltaMedianStations} stations against {phasenetMedianStations}. We own the tilt: the tier bars come from PhaseNet's located public events and the station corrections from PhaseNet's picks on them, though H1's threshold search on a common scale without corrections, over the six hours with the most public-catalog events, also found no strict STA/LTA event at any setting it tried (`baseline_tuning_b.json` in the run directory; a coordinate search, not exhaustive). *(On the card, the "STA/LTA strict events" row shows {strictStalta} of {staltaCandidates}; the {strictPhasenet} is not on the card: it is a rerun with one statics table for every event, so it differs slightly from the STRICT counter; never say the two side by side without that.)* |
| 12 | Why 3D? | The question is where underground, and depth is the dimension the public catalog is weakest in. Plan view is one key away. |
| 13 | Isn't this established seismology? | The methods are established; we're not claiming a new algorithm. We built an open, near-real-time product over public data: ingestion, association, local relocation, quality scoring, catalog comparison and an inspectable interface. High-resolution geothermal catalogs usually depend on dedicated arrays; we test how much of the seismicity, and at what depths, the public stream alone can surface. The Saturday depth call passed on amended criteria (`docs/lanes/H2-seismology.md`, "Depth call"): that licenses showing depths as located, not reading a pattern into them (no relative relocation was done). |
| 14 | What's your false-positive rate? | There's no ground truth for events the public catalog lacks, so no true rate. Our proxy is a null test: shift each station's pick times by its own random offset, which destroys the timing between stations, and rerun. That yields about {meanChanceEvents} chance events per scramble and none at strict quality in any of the {nShuffles} scrambles, so the chance floor sits in the lower tiers, not in strict; don't set it against all {candidateCount} (Q36). |
| 15 | What's your external validation? | Less than we'd like, and we say so. Public-catalog recall is a sanity check, not a sensitivity measure: every public event here was located on at least {minMatchedStations} stations (the median candidate has {medianStations}), and even STA/LTA recovers {staltaRecoveredPublic} of {publicCatalogCount}. Depth agreement with the same-day public catalog is held out per event but sits in that catalog's frame by construction (Q23); the synthetic test, the null test and the STA/LTA baseline are internal checks. Published FORGE catalogs cover other dates and places, so they're context, not a check, and we ran no second site. |
| 16 | Could these belong to Cape Station instead of FORGE? | We don't know, and we don't attribute to either. Several operations share the region; we show cited facility locations and our locations with their errors. Attribution needs operator data we don't have. |
| 17 | Why call this AI? | The detection step is a deep network trained on large labeled seismogram sets, and it's what makes small events visible. The rest is physics. We'd call it ML-assisted monitoring. |
| 18 | What did you build during HackGT? | Everything in the repo; git history starts at the kickoff commit. Pre-event work was research into which data and methods exist. No code, fixtures or outputs came over. |
| 19 | How near-real-time is it? | {latency} minutes for the last two hours on this laptop (only if measured). |
| 20 | Why that day ({windowLabel})? | It's a day with public-catalog activity in the region, which gives us reference events to validate against. |
| 21 | What about the high-rate borehole sensors? | We don't blindly resample. Each sensor type gets its own profile with explicit anti-aliasing, and we A/B'd a time-stretch variant to keep the high-frequency band. |
| 22 | Which velocity model? | A published FORGE 1D model from the Geothermal Data Repository, with a public 3D Cape/FORGE model as the upgrade path. The source is recorded in every run. |
| 23 | Aren't your depths just an artifact of the velocity model? | Uncorrected, they'd carry its errors: the 1D velocity model can't capture the region's lateral velocity changes, and our first-pass locations sat well off the catalog's. We correct with station timing terms fit at the public events' catalog hypocentres (Run details: `locator.statics.config.mode` is `referenceEvents`), each public event left out of its own term, so our absolute depths are in the catalog's frame by construction; held out, the median depth difference from the catalog is {heldOutMedianAbsDzM} m. As a cross-check, a public 3D model with station corrections fit the same way put the events where the 1D model does, within that held-out scatter (`docs/lanes/H2-seismology.md`, LOC-07; not in Run details). |
| 24 | Why not use the catalogs from the private downhole arrays? | They aren't public in real time, and the question is what the public stream alone supports. Our reference is the public regional catalog, and we don't claim independence from it: it sets our tier bars and anchors our station corrections (Q4). |
| 25 | What if Live shows nothing? | It says "No high-confidence candidate events in this window." We never fake activity. |
| 26 | Couldn't trucks or drilling noise fool it? | Noise at one station won't associate across many stations with consistent moveout. Persistent noise at one pad could, and that's what tiers and the evidence drawer are for. |
| 27 | How good are the magnitudes? | They're a local magnitude, {magType}: typical error {looMae} magnitude units (leave-one-out) versus {nullModelMae} for a no-skill baseline (a model that gives each event the mean magnitude of the others), from {n} calibration events of one catalog magnitude type; {belowCalibratedRange} candidate magnitudes are extrapolated below the smallest calibration event. That's why we compute a b-value but deliberately don't quote it. If the gate had failed there would be no magnitudes on screen. |
| 28 | Does your catalog lower the completeness magnitude? | *(No G-R panel is in the shell; say "in the bundle".)* We don't claim that. The public curve counts only the catalog events of the calibration magnitude type ({magType}), too few for a completeness estimate; ours, `ML_cal`, continues to smaller magnitudes, but its low end is extrapolated and censored, so we describe the curve and quote no b-value from it. |
| 29 | Who would use this? | Operators have specialized monitoring; the public mostly doesn't. This is the public-data layer: an independently inspectable view for researchers, regulators, journalists and communities. |
| 30 | Does it scale to other sites? | It's config-driven: area, stations and velocity model. The real per-site cost is a velocity model and station QC. We haven't run it anywhere else. |
| 31 | Why PyOcto and not GaMMA? | PyOcto is fast on dense pick sets. Either would work; the associator isn't our contribution. |
| 32 | What's the biggest weakness? | Depth: public station geometry is sparse, and our absolute depths sit in the public catalog's frame through the station corrections. We'd rather say it before you do. |
| 33 | Why trust your tier thresholds? | They come from the public events we recovered, and they're stored in the run config. Open Run details. |
| 34 | Did you cherry-pick the hero event? | It's chosen by a fixed rule: the strict event with the most stations, ties broken by the smallest timing misfit. It's also in the public catalog, so you can check us against it; for one the catalog doesn't list, see Q37. Click any other dot. |
| 35 | Of your strict events, how many aren't in the public catalog? | {strictAdditionalCount} of the {strictQualityCount}. The other {strictMatchedCount} are public events we recovered, and they're the yardstick: the new ones clear the same per-metric bars, which only {strictJointShare} recovered public events clear all at once. |
| 36 | {meanChanceEvents} chance events against {candidateCount}: so a big share of yours is noise? | It's a stress test, not a false-positive rate. We shift every station's pick times by its own random offset, so the timing between stations is gone, and the pipeline still builds about {meanChanceEvents} events per scramble from coincidences, with a wide spread, but none at strict quality in any of the {nShuffles} scrambles. So the full view carries a chance floor, while no scramble reached strict; that's why strict is the count we stand behind. |
| 37 | The event you showed is in the public catalog. Show me one that isn't. | Fair: we start with a public one so you can check us. *(Press S and click a strict dot whose drawer has no "Matched" line.)* By the hero's own rule, the strict event the catalog doesn't list with the most stations is `{hiddenHeroId}`, right beside the hero; its drawer shows P and S picks lining up with the arrivals its location implies, like the hero's. |
| 38 | ±{medianVErrM} m depth, with a median of {medianStations} stations? | That's the best case, not a typical event: synthetic events on our real station geometry, every station with a P pick, located with the same velocity model that made them, so it measures precision, not accuracy. The drawer shows each event's own vertical error (±v) when it has one, strict events carry it as a halo, and events on fewer stations are much looser. Against the public catalog, each event held out of its own station corrections, the median depth difference is {heldOutMedianAbsDzM} m, and most of those differences are within the catalog's own stated depth error. |
| 39 | Your station corrections are fit at the catalog's hypocentres. Aren't your depths just the catalog's? | Our absolute frame is the catalog's, yes, by construction: each public event is left out of its own correction, but the corrections come from the others' catalog hypocentres, so average agreement with catalog depths is expected and we don't count it as validation. What's ours is the detection, and where each candidate sits relative to the public events in that frame, from our own picks, with its own location error. The corrections are calibrated near the public events and less certain far from them, and we ran no relative relocation, so we read no pattern into those positions. |
| 40 | The time histogram swings a lot. Is activity picking up at certain hours? | The scrubber counts detections per bin, not an activity rate. ## Devpost outline

1. **Title + tagline:** "Hidden Quakes: what the public can't see beneath Utah's geothermal frontier."
2. **Thumbnail:** semi-transparent terrain, the geothermal reference, public points in white, strict candidate events in amber below, and a "{publicCatalogCount} PUBLIC → {candidateCount} RECOVERED" counter. It has to read with no video; if it looks like random dots, redesign.
3. **Inspiration:** enhanced geothermal is expanding; operators see underground and the public doesn't.
4. **What it does:** the 10-second pitch, then the three interactions (reveal, evidence, time).
5. **How we built it:** pipeline diagram, stack, data sources.
6. **Validation:** recall, strict count, depth resolution, plus baseline and null test if measured. Numbers copied from the validation panel.
7. **Limits (its own heading):** candidate events, sparse public geometry, no attribution, pretrained picker.
8. **Challenges:** borehole sample rates, depth credibility, datum handling.
9. **What's next:** other public-network sites, relative relocation, continuous live operation.
10. **Built with:** Python (ObsPy, SeisBench, PyOcto, scikit-fmm, SciPy, Pydantic, FastAPI), TypeScript (Next.js, React, three.js, React Three Fiber, zustand); the full list is in `docs/demo/devpost.md`.
11. **Links:** deployed URL, repo, 2-minute video.
12. **HackGT compliance line:** what was pre-event research vs what was built during the event.
